Today we are presenting Chagee: a tea brand most of you know, where you order in the app and collect at the counter, or have it delivered to your location.

Our scope is Chagee Singapore: its management and its Singapore customers. Singapore outlets are company-owned, not franchised, as our project owner confirmed, so the counter, the roster and the equipment are Chagee's own decisions. If asked about the group: across Chagee worldwide, most FY 2025 net revenue came from franchising, about {{facts.revenue_share_franchise_fy2025|pct}}, from a secondary source; that does not describe Singapore.

Why Chagee: many stores, managed by one company with one data system and lots of data, by an anonymous Chagee source; app-centric, and its Form 20-F says it collects personal and transactional data; fast-growing and playful, expanding rapidly, with room for improvements with AI. And we were fortunate to interview a Chagee staff member, who asked not to be named, about the problems they actually face.

Now follow one cup from farm to customer. Upstream is planned ahead: tea leaves come from Yunnan through standard channels, only fresh milk and fruit are bought locally (YF Chuhai), and Chagee already plans restocking from forecasts (Form 20-F). Downstream is where the data is: the outlet makes and hands over each cup, and the Chagee app links the outlet to the last mile, whether the customer collects in store or has it delivered, for example by Grab. Which of these Chagee keeps, and in how much detail, is ours to confirm. Upstream data and decisions sit with Chagee corporate; downstream data is analysed and decisions are made by Chagee Singapore, which is why our project focuses there.

So we focus on the downstream operational challenges, some of the most pressing issues for Chagee Singapore's management. That leads into the executive summary. About one minute.

    This is the solution, as an illustrative prototype; the rules are ours, not Chagee's. Two parts, kept apart: squads, a product design, and personalisation, the AI part, which suggests a drink from each member's own choices and lets a gift's recipient choose privately. Click through: a member sees their progress and starts a squad, invites friends, then chooses a drink, with a suggestion they can accept, customise or dismiss and feedback such as not for me.         Appendix · How the site forecast works (slide 09)

        DBSCAN finds where searches bunch up; a proximity model turns a pin into cups

        DBSCAN in four moves · toy example
        1. 1Radius ε. Two searches are neighbours if they are within ε of each other (the cluster-radius slider).
